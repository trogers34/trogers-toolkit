Peter Abelard

1079–1142 · Medieval

Also known as Pierre Abélard

French philosopher, theologian, and teacher

The most famous teacher of 12th-century Paris, whose logical method shaped scholastic theology, whose affair with Héloïse ended in his castration, and whose teaching was twice condemned.

Born
1079 · Le Pallet, near Nantes, Brittany[2][3]
Died
21 April 1142 · Priory of Saint-Marcel, near Chalon-sur-Saône, Burgundy[2][1]

What they did

  • Drew students from across Europe to Paris and helped make it the center of learning from which the university grew.[2][3]
  • In Sic et Non ('Yes and No') set apparently contradictory statements of the church fathers side by side with rules for reconciling them, a method that shaped scholastic theology.[2][3]
  • Argued in logic for a middle position on universals, that general terms are meaningful words rather than real things, a key step toward 'nominalism'.[2][3]
  • Taught in his Ethics (Know Yourself) that sin lies in consent and intention rather than in outward acts.[2]
  • Taught that Christ's death saves above all by revealing God's love and awakening love in return (often called the 'moral influence' view of the atonement).[2][3]
  • Seduced Héloïse, a gifted young woman his student and in his care in her uncle's house; he admitted in his own account that he planned the seduction.[1][2]
  • Wrote the Historia Calamitatum, an autobiography, and exchanged letters with Héloïse that are among the most famous in literature; he also wrote hymns and a rule for her convent.[1][3]

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of Peter Abelard supporting or carrying out persecution of others; he was himself condemned twice. His Dialogue of a Philosopher with a Jew and a Christian gives the Jew a sympathetic voice in describing the mistreatment Jews suffered among Christians.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Abelard suffered a violent attack and two church condemnations, the second pressed by Bernard of Clairvaux.

Excommunications

Abelard was not formally excommunicated, but his teaching was condemned twice.

c. 1117–1118

Castration by Fulbert's men

Believing Abelard had abandoned Héloïse after sending her to the convent at Argenteuil, her uncle Fulbert, a canon of Notre-Dame, had men bribe Abelard's servant and castrate him in his sleep. Two of the attackers were caught and blinded and castrated in punishment.[1][2]

1121

Council of Soissons

Rival teachers Alberic of Reims and Lotulf of Novara brought charges against his treatise on the Trinity. A council under the papal legate Conan of Praeneste condemned it without a proper hearing; Abelard was made to burn it with his own hands and was confined for a time in the monastery of Saint-Médard.[1][2]

1140–1141

Condemnation at Sens

Bernard of Clairvaux and William of Saint-Thierry drew up a list of errors from Abelard's works. At the Council of Sens Abelard refused to debate and appealed to Rome, but the bishops condemned the propositions, and Pope Innocent II confirmed the condemnation, ordered his books burned, and sentenced him to perpetual silence and confinement in a monastery.[2][3]
