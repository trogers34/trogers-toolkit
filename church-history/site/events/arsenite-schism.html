Arsenite Schism

c. 1262–1310 · Medieval

A split in the Byzantine church after Patriarch Arsenios excommunicated Emperor Michael VIII for blinding the boy emperor John IV, and was deposed for it; his followers refused communion with the official patriarchs for over 40 years.

Began
c. 1262 (excommunication of Michael VIII)[2][3]
Ended
September 1310 (reconciliation)[2][3]
Place
Constantinople and the Byzantine Empire

Background

  • Michael VIII Palaiologos, co-emperor and regent for the child John IV Laskaris, recaptured Constantinople in 1261 and then had John IV, aged 11, blinded on Christmas Day 1261 to remove him as a rival.[2][1]

What happened

  • Patriarch Arsenios Autoreianos excommunicated Michael VIII for the crime.[3][2]
  • Michael had a synod depose Arsenios in 1265 on charges including communion with the sultan of Iconium, and exiled him to the island of Proconnesus, where he died in 1273.[2][3]
  • Many monks and laypeople, the 'Arsenites', refused to recognize his successors Germanos III and Joseph I, who absolved the emperor in 1267, and formed a separate party in church and politics.[3][2]
  • The split overlapped with resistance to Michael VIII's union with Rome at the Second Council of Lyon (1274).[3][1]

Key participants

  • Arsenios Autoreianos Patriarch of Constantinople who excommunicated the emperor
  • Michael VIII Palaiologos Byzantine emperor who blinded John IV
  • John IV Laskaris Boy emperor blinded and imprisoned
  • Joseph I Patriarch who absolved Michael VIII
  • Andronikos II Palaiologos Emperor who worked to reconcile the Arsenites

Outcomes and significance

  • Andronikos II, Michael's son, brought Arsenios's remains back to the capital (1284), visited the blind John IV to seek forgiveness (1290), and finally reconciled the Arsenites in 1310.[2]
  • The schism showed a patriarch using excommunication against an emperor and the emperor using a synod to remove him, a recurring pattern in Byzantium.[3]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

25 December 1261 onward

Blinding and imprisonment of John IV

On Michael VIII's orders the 11-year-old emperor John IV Laskaris was blinded and imprisoned in a fortress in Bithynia, where he lived for decades.[2][1]

1265–1273

Deposition and exile of Arsenios

The patriarch was deposed by a synod packed with the emperor's supporters and exiled; Arsenite clergy and monks who refused communion with his successors were harassed, imprisoned, or exiled.[2][3]
