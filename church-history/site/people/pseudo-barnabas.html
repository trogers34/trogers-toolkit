Pseudo-Barnabas

c. 70–c. 140 · Early Church

Also known as The anonymous author of the Epistle of Barnabas

Anonymous early Christian teacher; author of the Epistle of Barnabas

The unknown author of the Epistle of Barnabas, a treatise traditionally credited to Paul's companion Barnabas but almost certainly written by someone else between about 96 and 135. The dates shown on the timeline are placeholders for an unknown life. The author is one of the "Apostolic Fathers," the earliest Christian writers after the New Testament (c. 90–150).

What they did

  • Wrote the Epistle of Barnabas, which reads the Jewish Scriptures allegorically as pointing to Christ, for example interpreting the food laws and circumcision as spiritual rather than literal commands.[1][2]
  • Included a "Two Ways" teaching (the way of light and the way of darkness), a moral instruction also found in the Didache, an early church manual.[1][4]
  • Argued that the covenant belonged to Christians and not to the Jews, who, he claimed, had misunderstood their own Law. This is one of the earliest strongly anti-Jewish Christian writings.[2][4]
  • Was highly regarded in the early church: Clement of Alexandria quoted it as the work of the apostle Barnabas, and it was copied in the 4th-century Codex Sinaiticus after the New Testament.[2][3]
