Old Calendarist Schism

1924–1935 · Modern

The split that followed the adoption of the Revised Julian calendar by the Church of Greece and other Orthodox churches in 1924, when many faithful refused to change and formed separate Old Calendarist churches.

Began
10/23 March 1924 (Church of Greece changes calendar)[1][2]
Ended
1935 (first bishops join; the split continues)[1]
Place
Greece, Romania, Cyprus, and the Orthodox diaspora

Background

  • A Pan-Orthodox Congress in Constantinople in 1923, called by Patriarch Meletios IV, proposed the Revised Julian calendar, which matches the Gregorian calendar for fixed feasts while keeping the traditional date of Easter.[1][2]

What happened

  • The Church of Greece adopted the new calendar in March 1924, as did Constantinople and soon Romania; the churches of Russia, Serbia, Jerusalem, and Mount Athos kept the old Julian calendar.[1][2]
  • Many Greek laypeople and monks refused, saying the change had been made without a general council and to suit the West; they formed Old Calendarist communities, at first without bishops.[1]
  • In 1935 three Greek bishops, led by Chrysostomos of Florina, joined them; the movement later divided into several rival synods.[1]

Key participants

  • Meletios IV Metaxakis Patriarch of Constantinople who promoted the change
  • Chrysostomos Papadopoulos Archbishop of Athens who introduced it in Greece
  • Chrysostomos Kavourides of Florina Bishop who led the Old Calendarists from 1935

Outcomes and significance

  • Orthodox churches still use two calendars for fixed feasts; Old Calendarist churches remain outside communion with the mainstream churches.[1][2]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

The Greek state backed the official church against the Old Calendarists in the 1920s–1950s.

1924–1950s

State action against Old Calendarists in Greece

Greek governments, at the official church's request, at times arrested Old Calendarist clergy, closed or seized their churches, and forcibly shaved and defrocked priests; Bishop Chrysostomos of Florina was exiled in 1951.[1][2]
