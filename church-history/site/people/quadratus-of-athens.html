Quadratus of Athens

c. 70–c. 130 · Early Church

Also known as Quadratus the Apologist

Christian apologist (defender of the faith); possibly a bishop of Athens

Author of the earliest known Christian apology, a written defense of the faith presented to Emperor Hadrian around 125. Only a single sentence survives. He is counted with both the Apostolic Fathers and the later Apologists.

What they did

  • Wrote the first known Christian apology, a defense of Christianity addressed to Emperor Hadrian, beginning the tradition of 2nd-century apologetic writing.[2][3]
  • His one surviving fragment, preserved by Eusebius, says that some of those Jesus healed or raised from the dead had lived on "even to our own times."[2][1]
  • Eusebius reports that his apology was still in circulation in Eusebius's own day, about two centuries later.[2]
  • Some scholars have suggested that the anonymous Epistle to Diognetus may be his lost apology, but this is unproven.[1][3]

Persecution, arrests, and executions

No reliable record of persecution of Quadratus survives. Later traditions about him combine several different men named Quadratus (an apologist, a prophet, and a bishop of Athens), and they cannot be confirmed. His apology may have been written in response to hostility toward Christians.

Excommunications

No excommunication or condemnation of him is recorded.
