Baptist Churches: Beginnings and Splits

1609–1991 · Reformation

How the Baptist churches began among English Separatists in exile in 1609 and divided over theology, missions, slavery, race, and the authority of Scripture.

Began
1609[2][3]
Ended
1991[3][4]
Place
Amsterdam, England, and North America

Background

  • Baptists grew out of English Puritan Separatism. They are distinct in origin from the Continental Anabaptists of 1525, though Smyth's group had contact with Dutch Mennonites.[2][3]

What happened

  • John Smyth's congregation in Amsterdam adopted believers' baptism in 1609; Thomas Helwys led part of it back to London in 1612, forming the 'General Baptists', who held that Christ died for all.[2][3]
  • Calvinistic 'Particular Baptists' arose in London in the 1630s, adopted immersion, and issued the London Confessions of 1644 and 1689.[2][3]
  • In America Baptists were a persecuted minority until the Revolution, then grew rapidly in the Great Awakenings, especially in the South and among enslaved and free Black Americans.[2][4]
  • Disputes over mission societies, slavery, race, and liberal theology repeatedly divided Baptists, who have no central authority over local congregations.[3][4]

Key participants

  • John Smyth Founder of the first English Baptist congregation (Amsterdam)
  • Thomas Helwys Founder of the first Baptist church in England
  • Roger Williams Founder of Rhode Island and the first American Baptist church
  • William Carey Founder of the Baptist Missionary Society (1792) and missionary to India
  • Charles Haddon Spurgeon Leading 19th-century Particular Baptist preacher; left the Baptist Union in the Down-Grade Controversy (1887)
  • Billy Graham Southern Baptist evangelist

Outcomes and significance

  • Baptists became one of the largest Protestant families, with the Southern Baptist Convention the largest Protestant denomination in the United States.[3][4]
  • Their early insistence on religious liberty and separation of church and state influenced the First Amendment and later democratic thought.[2][3]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

Early Baptists were persecuted by both church and state in England and colonial America.

1612–c. 1616

Imprisonment of Thomas Helwys

Helwys published A Short Declaration of the Mystery of Iniquity, the first English plea for full religious liberty, and sent a signed copy to King James I telling him he had no power over people's souls. He was imprisoned in Newgate and died there about 1616.[1][2]

1660–1672

Imprisonment of John Bunyan and Restoration penalties

After the Restoration, laws against nonconformist worship were used against Baptists; the Baptist preacher John Bunyan spent about 12 years in Bedford jail for unlicensed preaching, where he wrote Grace Abounding.[2][4]

1651

Whipping of Obadiah Holmes in Boston

Massachusetts authorities fined the Baptists John Clarke, Obadiah Holmes, and John Crandall for holding a service; Holmes refused to let his fine be paid and was publicly whipped with 30 lashes.[2]

1768–1776

Jailing of Baptist preachers in Virginia

Some 30 or more Baptist preachers were jailed in Anglican Virginia for preaching without licenses, some preaching through the bars to crowds outside.[2][3]
