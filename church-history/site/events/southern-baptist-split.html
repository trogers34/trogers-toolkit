Southern Baptist Split of 1845

1844–1845 · Modern

The division of American Baptists when Southern Baptists, refused the right to appoint slaveholders as missionaries, formed the Southern Baptist Convention.

Began
1844[2][3]
Ended
8–12 May 1845[2][4]
Place
Augusta, Georgia

Background

  • Since 1814 Baptists North and South had cooperated through the Triennial Convention for foreign missions and the American Baptist Home Mission Society, while abolitionist feeling grew in the North.[2][3]

What happened

  • In 1844 Georgia Baptists tested the Home Mission Society by proposing a slaveholder, James E. Reeve, as a missionary; the society declined to act on the nomination.[2]
  • The Alabama convention asked the foreign mission board whether it would appoint slaveholders; the board replied in December 1844 that it would not.[2][3]
  • In May 1845 about 300 delegates from Southern states met at Augusta and formed the Southern Baptist Convention, electing William B. Johnson of South Carolina as president.[2][4]

Key participants

  • William B. Johnson First president of the Southern Baptist Convention
  • Francis Wayland Northern Baptist leader who opposed slavery but regretted the split

Outcomes and significance

  • The SBC grew into the largest Protestant denomination in the United States.[3][4]
  • After emancipation, most Black Baptists in the South left white-led churches to form their own congregations and conventions.[2][4]
  • In 1995 the SBC adopted a resolution on racial reconciliation acknowledging that its founding was connected to slavery and apologizing to African Americans.[1][4]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

No one was persecuted by the split itself. The underlying issue was the enslavement of millions of Black Americans, which Southern Baptist leaders defended from Scripture at the time.
