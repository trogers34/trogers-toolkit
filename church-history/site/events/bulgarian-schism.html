Bulgarian Schism

1872–1945 · Modern

The break between the Patriarchate of Constantinople and the Bulgarian Exarchate, which Constantinople declared schismatic in 1872 for organizing the church by nationality ('ethnophyletism').

Began
September 1872 (exarchate declared schismatic)[1][3]
Ended
22 February 1945 (schism lifted)[1][3]
Place
Constantinople and Bulgarian lands of the Ottoman Empire

Background

  • Bulgarians under Ottoman rule resented the Greek-speaking higher clergy appointed by Constantinople and campaigned for their own church as part of a national revival.[2][3]
  • In 1870 Sultan Abdülaziz issued a decree (firman) creating a Bulgarian Exarchate with authority over Bulgarian dioceses, without the Patriarch's agreement.[3][2]

What happened

  • In 1872 the Bulgarians elected Antim I as exarch, and he declared the exarchate's independence from the Patriarchate in May.[3][1]
  • A council in Constantinople in September 1872 condemned 'phyletism' or ethnophyletism (organizing the church by ethnic group) as heresy and declared the exarchate schismatic.[1][2]
  • The Russian Orthodox Church and some others did not break communion with the Bulgarians, and the schism became tied to rivalry between Greek and Bulgarian nationalism in Macedonia and Thrace.[1][3]

Key participants

  • Antim I First Bulgarian exarch
  • Anthimos VI Patriarch of Constantinople at the 1872 council
  • Abdülaziz Ottoman sultan who created the exarchate

Outcomes and significance

  • Constantinople lifted the schism in 1945, recognizing the Bulgarian church as autocephalous, and recognized its patriarchate in 1961.[1][3]
  • The 1872 condemnation of ethnophyletism is still cited in Orthodox debates about national churches and diaspora jurisdictions.[1][2]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

The church dispute fed violent competition between Greek and Bulgarian nationalists over parishes and schools in Ottoman Macedonia, especially around 1900–1908, in which priests and teachers on both sides were killed.
