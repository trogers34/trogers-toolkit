Council of Ephesus

431 · Early Church

Also known as Third Ecumenical Council

The council at which Cyril of Alexandria had Nestorius of Constantinople deposed and Mary affirmed as Theotokos ('God-bearer'), held amid rival assemblies, imperial arrests, and bribery of the court.

Began
22 June 431[1][2]
Ended
Dissolved by the emperor c. September–October 431[1][3]
Place
Ephesus, Asia (near modern Selçuk, Turkey)

Background

  • Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople from 428, objected to calling Mary Theotokos ('God-bearer') and preferred Christotokos ('Christ-bearer'); Cyril of Alexandria accused him of dividing Christ into two persons.[3][4]
  • A Roman synod under Pope Celestine I condemned Nestorius in August 430 and asked Cyril to enforce it; Cyril added twelve anathemas that Nestorius and the bishops of Antioch rejected.[3][1]
  • Emperor Theodosius II summoned a council to Ephesus for Pentecost 431.[1]

What happened

  • On 22 June 431 Cyril opened the council with about 150 bishops, over the protest of the imperial commissioner Candidian and 68 bishops, before John of Antioch and the Syrian bishops arrived. That same day it deposed Nestorius, who refused to attend.[1][3]
  • John of Antioch arrived on 26 June and held a rival council of about 43 bishops with Candidian's support, which deposed Cyril and Memnon, bishop of Ephesus.[1][3]
  • Pope Celestine's legates arrived in July and endorsed Cyril's council.[1][2]
  • A letter from Capreolus of Carthage was read explaining that the African bishops could not come; Theodosius's invitation to Augustine had arrived after Augustine's death in August 430.[1]
  • Cyril's council also condemned Pelagian teaching and forbade composing any creed other than that of Nicaea (canon 7).[2]
  • In August Theodosius accepted both sides' depositions and had Cyril, Memnon, and Nestorius held under guard. Cyril's agents spent heavily on gifts to court officials; a list of these 'blessings' survives. Cyril escaped back to Alexandria, Nestorius was sent to his monastery near Antioch, and the emperor dissolved the council.[3][1]

Key participants

  • Theodosius II Eastern Roman emperor; summoned the council
  • Cyril of Alexandria Presided over the majority council
  • Nestorius Bishop of Constantinople; deposed
  • John of Antioch Led the rival council
  • Memnon of Ephesus Local bishop and Cyril's ally
  • Celestine I Bishop of Rome; sent legates who backed Cyril
  • Augustine of Hippo Invited by Theodosius II, but died before the invitation arrived

Outcomes and significance

  • In the Formula of Reunion (433) Cyril and John of Antioch were reconciled; John accepted Nestorius's deposition and Cyril accepted a statement of Christ's two natures.[3][4]
  • Calling Mary Theotokos became standard in Catholic and Orthodox teaching and devotion.[4][2]
  • The Church of the East in Persia never accepted the council and is still sometimes, inaccurately, called 'Nestorian'.[4]
  • The quarrel over how to read Cyril led directly to the Council of Ephesus of 449 and to Chalcedon (451).[3][1]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

No one was killed. The emperor's arrests in 431 hit both sides; the lasting penalties fell on Nestorius and his supporters.

August–September 431

Arrest of Cyril, Memnon, and Nestorius

The imperial official John, sent by Theodosius II, placed all three bishops under guard after the emperor accepted both councils' depositions. Cyril's party then won the court over, helped by large gifts to officials.[3][1]

435–c. 450

Exile of Nestorius and ban on his books

Theodosius II ordered Nestorius's books burned and his followers to be called 'Simonians', and exiled him first to Petra and then to the Great Oasis in Egypt, where he was caught up in raids by nomads.[3][4]
