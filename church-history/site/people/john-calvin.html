John Calvin

1509–1564 · Reformation

Also known as Jean Calvin (Jehan Cauvin)

French Reformer, theologian, and pastor of Geneva

French theologian whose Institutes of the Christian Religion and work in Geneva shaped the Reformed and Presbyterian churches.

Born
10 July 1509 · Noyon, Picardy, France[3][6]
Died
27 May 1564 · Geneva[3][6]

What they did

  • Wrote the Institutes of the Christian Religion, expanded from a short handbook (1536) to a full system of theology (1559), the most influential work of Reformed Protestant theology.[1][3]
  • Organized the Genevan church under the Ecclesiastical Ordinances (1541), with pastors, teachers, elders, and deacons, and a Consistory that disciplined moral conduct; the model spread to France, Scotland, the Netherlands, and beyond.[3][5]
  • Taught God's sovereignty in salvation, including double predestination, which became a defining doctrine of Reformed theology.[1][6]
  • Wrote commentaries on most of the Bible and preached several times a week in Geneva for over two decades.[3][6]
  • Served as pastor to French refugees in Strasbourg (1538–41), where he worked with Martin Bucer and took part in religious colloquies with Catholic and Lutheran theologians.[3]
  • Founded the Genevan Academy (1559), which trained pastors sent into France; Geneva sent over 100 ministers to France by 1562.[3][6]
  • Wrote against the Council of Trent in Acts of the Council of Trent with the Antidote (1547).[3]
  • Pressed the heresy prosecution of Michael Servetus, who was burned in Geneva in 1553, and defended the execution in print; he had earlier supplied evidence that helped the Catholic Inquisition at Vienne prosecute Servetus.[4][3]
  • Supported the Geneva Consistory's strict enforcement of morals and the punishment of opponents, including the execution of Jacques Gruet (1547) and the banishment of Jerome Bolsec (1551) for attacking his teaching on predestination.[5][3]

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Calvin was never imprisoned for his faith (an imprisonment at Noyon in 1534 is sometimes reported but may concern another man of the same name). He fled France during the crackdown on Protestants in 1533–34 and was expelled from Geneva in 1538.

Excommunications

No papal bull excommunicating Calvin by name is known, but as a Protestant he was outside the Catholic Church and all his works were banned.

November 1533 – 1535

Flight from France

After the rector Nicolas Cop gave a reforming address at the University of Paris, which Calvin may have helped write, the Parlement of Paris moved against both men and Calvin fled the city. After the Affair of the Placards (October 1534), when anti-Mass posters appeared across France, Francis I's government burned Protestants and Calvin left France for Basel.[3][6]

23 April 1538

Expulsion from Geneva

After Calvin and Farel refused to follow the city's orders on church practice and to give communion while the city was divided, the councils of Geneva gave them three days to leave.[3][6]
