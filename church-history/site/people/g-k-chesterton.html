G. K. Chesterton

1874–1936 · Modern

Also known as Gilbert Keith Chesterton

English writer, journalist, and Christian apologist; convert to Catholicism

Prolific English writer whose books Orthodoxy and The Everlasting Man, and his Father Brown detective stories, made him one of the best-known Christian apologists of the 20th century.

Died

What they did

  • Wrote Orthodoxy (1908), an account of how he came to accept Christianity, which remains one of the most widely read works of Christian apologetics.[1][5]
  • Wrote The Everlasting Man (1925), a reply to H. G. Wells's Outline of History that C. S. Lewis later credited with helping lead him to Christianity.[5][6]
  • Created the priest-detective Father Brown, modeled partly on his friend Father John O'Connor, in five collections of stories (1911–35).[5][6]
  • Was received into the Catholic Church in 1922 by Father O'Connor after many years as an Anglican, and wrote widely about the faith afterward, including biographies of Francis of Assisi (1923) and Thomas Aquinas (1933).[5][3]
  • With Hilaire Belloc promoted distributism, an economic program of widespread small property ownership as an alternative to both capitalism and socialism, through G.K.'s Weekly.[5][6]
  • Wrote some 80 books and thousands of essays and newspaper columns, and held public debates with George Bernard Shaw, H. G. Wells, and others.[5][6]
  • Wrote about Jews in ways that drew charges of antisemitism, during and after the Marconi scandal (1912–13), when his brother Cecil was convicted of libelling the businessman Godfrey Isaacs; in The New Jerusalem (1920) he proposed that Jews in England wear distinctive dress. In the 1930s he condemned Hitler's persecution of the Jews.[5][6]

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of Chesterton carrying out or supporting the persecution of anyone. He did write about Jews in ways widely criticized as antisemitic, and in The New Jerusalem (1920) proposed that Jews in England wear distinctive dress; in the 1930s he condemned Hitler's persecution of the Jews.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Chesterton was never arrested, tried, or persecuted. His brother Cecil was prosecuted and fined for criminal libel in 1913 over the Marconi affair, an action Chesterton strongly opposed but which did not target him.

Excommunications

Chesterton was never excommunicated or disciplined by any church. In 2019 the Catholic bishop of Northampton, Peter Doyle, declined to open a cause for his beatification, citing among other reasons the lack of a local cult and concerns about his writings on Jews.
