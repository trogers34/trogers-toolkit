Rod Rosenbladt

1942–2024 · Modern

Also known as Rodney Rosenbladt; 'Dad Rod'

Lutheran theologian and apologist; professor at Concordia University Irvine and original co-host of the White Horse Inn

A confessional Lutheran theologian who taught theology at Concordia University Irvine for decades and, from 1990, was one of the four original hosts of the White Horse Inn radio programme with Michael Horton, Kim Riddlebarger, and Ken Jones. A student of John Warwick Montgomery, he combined evidential apologetics with a strong emphasis on law and gospel, best known through his talk The Gospel for Those Broken by the Church. Late in life he helped found the 1517 organization with his son Ted.

What they did

  • Taught theology for decades at Concordia University Irvine, a Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod school, mentoring students such as the historian Adam Francisco.[10][7]
  • Was one of the four original hosts of the White Horse Inn (from 1990), a radio programme linked to Michael Horton's Christians United for Reformation that urged American evangelicals to recover Reformation theology; he was its Lutheran voice alongside Reformed co-hosts.[3][6]
  • Gave the widely circulated talk The Gospel for Those Broken by the Church, addressed to people hurt or exhausted by legalistic churches, stressing that Christ's forgiveness is for sinners and failures.[2][4]
  • Taught evidential Christian apologetics in the tradition of his teacher John Warwick Montgomery, arguing from the historical evidence for the resurrection; he is reported to have taught with Montgomery and Walter Martin at the Simon Greenleaf School of Law.[6][9]
  • Wrote Christ Alone (1999) in Crossway's Today's Issues series, edited by James Montgomery Boice; it was reissued by 1517 Publishing.[1]
  • Co-founded the 1517 organization with his son Ted, which publishes books, podcasts, and conferences in the confessional Lutheran law-and-gospel tradition.[7][10]
  • Wrote for Modern Reformation magazine and was remembered by colleagues as 'Dad Rod', a mentor to many younger pastors and writers.[4][5]

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of Rosenbladt supporting or carrying out persecution of anyone.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Rosenbladt was never arrested, tried, or persecuted. The White Horse Inn drew sharp theological criticism, for example from the Trinity Foundation, which attacked the programme's teaching; this was published disagreement, not persecution.

Excommunications

Rosenbladt was never excommunicated, deposed, or disciplined. He moved from the American Lutheran tradition into the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod and remained a minister in good standing.
