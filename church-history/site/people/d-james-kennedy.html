D. James Kennedy

1930–2007 · Modern

Also known as Dennis James Kennedy

Presbyterian pastor, broadcaster, founder of Evangelism Explosion, and Christian Right leader

Founding pastor of Coral Ridge Presbyterian Church in Fort Lauderdale, whose Evangelism Explosion method trained millions in personal evangelism and whose broadcasts and organizations made him a leading voice of the Christian Right.

Born
3 November 1930 · Augusta, Georgia, United States[4][3]
Died
5 September 2007 · Fort Lauderdale, Florida, United States[4]
Heart failure, months after a cardiac arrest in December 2006

What they did

  • Built Coral Ridge Presbyterian Church from a handful of members in 1959 into one of the largest Presbyterian congregations in the United States; it later joined the Presbyterian Church in America.[4][3]
  • Developed Evangelism Explosion, a method of training church members to share their faith using two diagnostic questions; it spread to churches in every country in the world.[1][4]
  • Reached millions through The Coral Ridge Hour television program and Truths That Transform radio broadcast.[4]
  • Founded Westminster Academy (1971), Knox Theological Seminary (1989), the Center for Christian Statesmanship in Washington (1995), and the Center for Reclaiming America (1996).[4][3]
  • Served on the board of the Moral Majority and campaigned against abortion and homosexuality and for the view that America was founded as a Christian nation; critics, including Americans United for Separation of Church and State, opposed his political activism.[3][4]
  • His Center for Reclaiming America sponsored the 1998 'Truth in Love' newspaper campaign promoting the claim that gay people could change their orientation, which was widely criticized.[4][3]
  • His ministry's 2006 documentary Darwin's Deadly Legacy, linking Darwin's theory to Nazism, was condemned by the Anti-Defamation League as trivializing the Holocaust.[4]

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of D. James Kennedy carrying out persecution of others. He campaigned politically against homosexuality and abortion, and his ministry promoted the claim that gay people could change their orientation, positions widely criticized by gay-rights and civil-liberties groups; this was political advocacy, not persecution.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Kennedy was never arrested or persecuted. His political activism drew strong public criticism from civil-liberties, gay-rights, and Jewish organizations.

Excommunications

Kennedy was never excommunicated or disciplined by any church.
