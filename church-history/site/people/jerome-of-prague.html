Jerome of Prague

c. 1379–1416 · Medieval

Also known as Jeroným Pražský

Czech philosopher, reformer, and ally of Jan Hus

A brilliant, restless scholar who brought John Wycliffe's writings from Oxford to Prague and became Jan Hus's closest ally. Arrested on his way home from the Council of Constance, he recanted under pressure, then withdrew his recantation before the council and was burned in 1416. An Italian humanist who watched his trial, Poggio Bracciolini, praised his eloquence and courage.

Born
c. 1379 (estimates range from the late 1360s to about 1380) · Prague, Kingdom of Bohemia[3][4]

What they did

  • Brought John Wycliffe's philosophical and theological writings from Oxford to Bohemia, where they shaped Jan Hus and the Czech reform movement.[3][4]
  • Was a leading philosophical realist and debater who earned master's degrees at several universities, including Prague, Paris, Cologne, and Heidelberg; his 'Shield of Faith' diagram of the Trinity was used in his disputations.[3]
  • Supported the Czech cause at the University of Prague and the 1409 Decree of Kutná Hora, which gave Bohemian masters three votes out of four.[3][4]
  • Was Jan Hus's closest ally, joining his campaign against the sale of indulgences in 1412 and following him to Constance in 1415.[3][2]
  • Recanted under pressure in September 1415, accepting Hus's condemnation, but in May 1416 publicly withdrew the recantation, praised Hus and Wycliffe, and accepted death.[1][3]
  • The humanist Poggio Bracciolini, a papal secretary who watched his defence, wrote a famous letter to Leonardo Bruni comparing his courage to that of the ancient philosophers.[1][4]
  • Is remembered by Czechs as a national martyr alongside Hus; his death helped spark the Hussite revolt.[3][4]

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of Jerome ordering or supporting judicial persecution. His opponents at Constance charged him with violence against friars and clergy who opposed the reform party in Prague; these charges come from hostile witnesses, and how much is true is uncertain.

c. 1410–1412

Alleged violence against friars in Prague (charges at Constance)

Carried it out

Against: Friars and clergy in Prague who preached for indulgences or against the reformers

Among the articles brought against him at Constance were claims that he struck a friar, threw a monk into the Vltava, and led crowds that intimidated preachers of indulgences. The charges came from his opponents; Jerome denied some of them, and historians treat them as partly exaggerated.[3]

Outcome: Used as evidence against him at his trial.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Jerome was arrested by church authorities several times, excommunicated, imprisoned for nearly a year at Constance, and burned. Responsibility lay with the Council of Constance and the secular authorities who carried out its sentence.

Excommunications

Jerome was excommunicated more than once and condemned as a heretic by the Council of Constance. The Catholic Church has never revoked the sentence; Pope John Paul II expressed regret in 1999 for Hus's death, but did not formally rehabilitate Hus or Jerome.

1410

Arrest in Vienna

While in Vienna he was arrested and tried by the bishop of Passau's officials on suspicion of Wycliffite heresy. He escaped before the case ended.[3]

April 1415 – May 1416

Arrest and imprisonment at Constance

He went to Constance in April 1415 without a safe-conduct to support Hus, left when he saw the danger, and was arrested at Hirsau. He was brought back to Constance in chains and held in harsh conditions; under pressure he recanted in September 1415.[2][3]

1416

Condemnation and burning

On 26 May 1416 he withdrew his recantation before the council. On 30 May the council condemned him as a relapsed heretic and handed him to the secular power; he was burned the same day.[1][3]
