Papias of Hierapolis

c. 60–c. 130 · Early Church

Bishop of Hierapolis in Phrygia; collector of traditions about Jesus

Early 2nd-century bishop of Hierapolis who gathered oral traditions from people who had known Jesus' followers. His lost work survives only in quotations, including the earliest statements about how the Gospels of Mark and Matthew were written. He is one of the "Apostolic Fathers," the earliest Christian writers after the New Testament (c. 90–150).

What they did

  • Wrote the Exposition of the Sayings of the Lord in five books, now lost except for quotations in later writers, especially Irenaeus and Eusebius.[1][2]
  • Preserved the earliest tradition about the Gospel of Mark: that Mark had been Peter's interpreter and wrote down accurately, though not in order, what he remembered of Peter's teaching.[2][3]
  • Also said that Matthew collected the sayings of Jesus "in the Hebrew language," a statement still debated by scholars.[2][3]
  • Said he preferred to learn from people who had known the apostles and from "the elder John" rather than from books. Irenaeus said Papias had heard the apostle John; Eusebius argued he meant a different John, "the Elder."[2][3]
  • Taught a literal thousand-year reign of Christ on earth, for which Eusebius dismissed him as "a man of very little intelligence."[2][1]

Words and sayings

Original 1

First recorded in their writing, or first worded this way by them.

  • "I did not think that information from books would help me as much as the word of a living and surviving voice." Preface to his Exposition, quoted by Eusebius, History of the Church 3.39[2][1]

Persecution, arrests, and executions

No reliable record of persecution of Papias survives. A late tradition that he was martyred at Pergamum probably comes from confusing him with a different martyr, Papylus.

Excommunications

No excommunication or condemnation of him is recorded. Eusebius criticized his millennial teaching, but that was a later historian's judgment, not a church sentence.
