Martin Chemnitz

1522–1586 · Reformation

Also known as "The Second Martin"

Lutheran theologian, superintendent of Braunschweig, principal author of the Formula of Concord

Leading Lutheran theologian of the generation after Luther, whose Examination of the Council of Trent was the major Protestant response to Trent, and who led the work on the Formula of Concord that united most German Lutherans.

Born
9 November 1522 · Treuenbrietzen, Brandenburg (Germany)[3][4]
Died
8 April 1586 · Braunschweig (Brunswick), Germany[3][4]

What they did

  • Wrote the Examination of the Council of Trent (4 volumes, 1565–1573), a point-by-point reply to Trent's decrees from Scripture and the Church Fathers, and the most important Lutheran response to the council.[1][3][4]
  • Was a principal author of the Formula of Concord (1577), which settled the disputes that divided Lutherans after Luther's death and was included in the Book of Concord (1580).[2][3]
  • Wrote The Two Natures in Christ (1570), a major work on the person of Christ and the Lord's Supper.[3]
  • Led the church of Braunschweig for nearly two decades, writing its church order and training its pastors.[3][4]
  • His Loci Theologici, lectures on Melanchthon's theology, were published after his death (1591), and he began a Harmony of the Gospels later completed by Polycarp Leyser and Johann Gerhard.[3]
  • Is often called "the second Martin," from a Catholic saying that "if the second Martin had not come, the first would hardly have stood."[3]

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Chemnitz was never arrested, exiled, or persecuted. He worked under the protection of Lutheran princes and the city of Braunschweig. He left Königsberg in 1553 amid the Osiandrian controversy, but that was a theological dispute among Lutherans, not persecution.

Excommunications

Chemnitz was never excommunicated by name. The Council of Trent's canons pronounced anathemas on Protestant doctrines in general, which Chemnitz answered in his Examination, but no church issued a sentence against him personally.
