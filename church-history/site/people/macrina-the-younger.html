Macrina the Younger

c. 327–c. 379 · Early Church

Also known as Saint Macrina; 'the Teacher'; sometimes called the fourth Cappadocian

Ascetic leader and teacher; elder sister of Basil the Great and Gregory of Nyssa

The eldest child of a devout Cappadocian family, who turned the family estate at Annesi into a community of ascetic women and men and, according to her brother Gregory of Nyssa, led Basil the Great himself to give up worldly ambition. Nearly everything known of her comes from Gregory's Life of Macrina and his dialogue On the Soul and the Resurrection, in which she is his teacher.

Born
c. 327 · Caesarea in Cappadocia[3][4]
Died
July 379 (some date it 380) · Annesi, Pontus, near the Iris River[1][3]
Illness (a fever), recorded at her deathbed by her brother Gregory

What they did

  • Led a mixed ascetic community at Annesi, where women of the family and former slaves lived, prayed, and worked as equals; her brother Peter led the men's house.[1][3]
  • Is portrayed by Gregory of Nyssa as the family's spiritual teacher and as a philosopher who guided both Basil and Gregory.[1][3]
  • Appears as Gregory's teacher in On the Soul and the Resurrection, a dialogue modelled on Plato's Phaedo, defending the immortality of the soul and the resurrection of the body.[2][3]
  • Raised and educated her youngest brother Peter, who became bishop of Sebaste.[1][3]
  • Cared for the poor during a famine, according to the Life, and gathered abandoned girls into the community.[1]
  • Is honoured as a saint in the Orthodox and Catholic churches; scholars note that Basil's own writings never mention her, so the picture of her depends on Gregory.[3][4]

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of Macrina supporting or carrying out persecution of anyone.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

No persecution of Macrina is recorded. Her grandmother, Macrina the Elder, had suffered in the persecution under Diocletian and Galerius, hiding in the forests of Pontus.

Excommunications

Macrina was never excommunicated or condemned.
