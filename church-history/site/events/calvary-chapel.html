Calvary Chapel: Beginnings and Splits

1965–2016 · Modern

How a small church in Costa Mesa, California, under Chuck Smith became the home of the Jesus Movement and a worldwide fellowship of churches, and how the Vineyard and later groups split from it.

Began
December 1965[2][3]
Ended
2016[3]
Place
Costa Mesa, California, then worldwide

Background

  • Chuck Smith, a pastor from the Foursquare denomination, took over a struggling independent church in Costa Mesa in 1965, as the counterculture grew in Southern California.[2][3]

What happened

  • With Lonnie Frisbee, Smith reached hippies and young people; the church held mass baptisms in the Pacific at Pirate's Cove, and thousands were converted in what became known as the Jesus Movement.[3][1]
  • Calvary Chapel combined verse-by-verse Bible teaching, informal dress, and contemporary worship music, spread through Maranatha! Music (1971).[2][3]
  • Pastors trained at Costa Mesa planted hundreds of churches in a loose fellowship rather than a denomination.[2]
  • Smith dismissed Frisbee over his ministry style, and later disagreements over charismatic practice led John Wimber's group to leave in 1982.[3][4]

Key participants

  • Chuck Smith Founding pastor of Calvary Chapel Costa Mesa
  • Lonnie Frisbee Jesus Movement evangelist; later left and died in 1993
  • John Wimber Founder of the Vineyard movement
  • Brian Brodersen Smith's son-in-law and successor at Costa Mesa

Outcomes and significance

  • Calvary Chapel grew to well over 1,000 affiliated churches and strongly influenced American evangelical worship and church style.[2][3]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

Calvary Chapel faced no persecution; its divisions came through disagreements among its leaders.
