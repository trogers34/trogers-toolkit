Athanasius of Alexandria

c. 296–373 · Early Church

Also known as Saint Athanasius the Great

Bishop (patriarch) of Alexandria, theologian

Bishop of Alexandria who spent nearly half a century defending the Nicene teaching that the Son is fully God against Arianism, enduring five exiles under four emperors.

Born
c. 296–298 · Alexandria, Egypt[2][5]
Died
2 May 373 · Alexandria, Egypt[3][5]

What they did

  • Defended the Nicene Creed's teaching that the Son is "of one substance" (homoousios) with the Father against Arius and his successors for nearly fifty years, often almost alone among leading bishops.[2][3][5]
  • Wrote On the Incarnation, a classic explanation of why God became human, and the Orations against the Arians.[1][2]
  • Wrote the Life of Antony (c. 360), a biography of the desert hermit that spread the ideal of monasticism across the Roman world.[2][5]
  • In his 39th Festal Letter (367), gave the first known list of exactly the 27 books now in the New Testament.[2][5]
  • Built close ties with the Egyptian monks, who sheltered him during his exiles.[3][2]
  • Is honored as a saint and Church Father in Orthodox, Catholic, Anglican, and Lutheran churches, and as a Doctor of the Church in the Catholic Church.[5]

  • "Athanasius against the world" (Athanasius contra mundum) A later description of him, not his words[5]

    A later Latin phrase summing up his lonely stand, not something he said.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Athanasius was exiled five times by four emperors, spending about seventeen of his forty-five years as bishop away from his see. He was repeatedly condemned by councils of bishops on the Arian or compromise side, and on one occasion soldiers broke into his church to seize him. He was never executed and died as bishop in Alexandria.

Excommunications

Athanasius was deposed and condemned several times by councils of bishops, mostly on the Arian or compromise side. All of these sentences were overturned or ignored, and he died in possession of his see. He is venerated as a saint in all major traditions.

335–337

Deposed at Tyre; first exile to Trier

His opponents accused him of violence against his clergy and even of murdering a bishop named Arsenius, who later turned up alive. The Council of Tyre, dominated by his opponents, deposed him. Emperor Constantine, after being told that Athanasius had threatened to stop Egypt's grain shipments to Constantinople, exiled him to Trier in Gaul.[3][5]

339–346

Second exile; replaced by Gregory of Cappadocia

Emperor Constantius II, who favored the anti-Nicene bishops, had Gregory of Cappadocia installed as bishop of Alexandria by force. Athanasius fled to Rome.[3][2]

February 356 – 362

Soldiers storm his church; third exile

After the councils of Arles (353) and Milan (355) condemned him under imperial pressure, troops under the military commander Syrianus broke into the Church of Theonas during a night vigil to arrest him. He escaped and hid for six years among the desert monks, while George of Cappadocia was installed as bishop.[3][5]

362–363

Fourth exile, under Julian the Apostate

The pagan emperor Julian, who had allowed exiled bishops to return, banished Athanasius again after he converted prominent pagans in Alexandria, calling him an enemy of the gods.[3][5]

365–366

Fifth exile, under Valens

Emperor Valens, who favored the anti-Nicene party, ordered bishops exiled under Constantius to be expelled again. Athanasius hid for about four months, reportedly in his family's tomb outside the city.[3][5]
