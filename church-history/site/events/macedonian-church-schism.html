Macedonian Orthodox Church Schism

1967–2022 · Modern

The 55-year break after the Macedonian Orthodox Church declared itself independent of the Serbian Orthodox Church in 1967, ended by its recognition in 2022.

Began
17–19 July 1967 (autocephaly declared at Ohrid)[3][4]
Ended
May–June 2022 (communion restored and autocephaly granted by Serbia)[1][4]
Place
Ohrid and Skopje (North Macedonia); Belgrade

Background

  • The Serbian church had governed the dioceses of Macedonia; in 1958, with support from Yugoslavia's communist government, it allowed a Macedonian church with its own head under the Serbian patriarch.[3][4]

What happened

  • In July 1967 a church council at Ohrid declared the Macedonian Orthodox Church autocephalous (fully independent), claiming the heritage of the medieval Archbishopric of Ohrid; the Yugoslav authorities supported the move.[3][4]
  • The Serbian church declared the Macedonian church schismatic and its bishops outside the church, and no other Orthodox church recognized it.[3][4]

Key participants

  • Dositej First head of the Macedonian Orthodox Church
  • Serbian Orthodox Church Mother church that declared the schism
  • Jovan Vraniškovski Bishop who left the Macedonian church to lead a Serbian-backed archbishopric; imprisoned
  • Bartholomew I Ecumenical Patriarch who received the church back into communion in 2022

Outcomes and significance

  • On 9 May 2022 the Ecumenical Patriarchate received the church back into communion under the name Archdiocese of Ohrid; the Serbian church recognized it later that month and granted it autocephaly in June 2022, though its name remains disputed.[1][4]

Violence, persecution, and who was responsible

2002–2006

Prosecution of Bishop Jovan Vraniškovski

After Jovan Vraniškovski accepted the Niš agreement and led an archbishopric under the Serbian church, the Macedonian church expelled him and the government of North Macedonia (then the Republic of Macedonia) prosecuted him. In 2004 he was imprisoned for 'inciting ethnic and religious hatred'; Amnesty International called him a prisoner of conscience.[2][4]
