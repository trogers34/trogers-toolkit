Clement of Rome

c. 35–c. 99 · Early Church

Also known as Pope Clement I; Saint Clement

Leader (traditionally bishop) of the church in Rome; author of 1 Clement

Leader of the church at Rome at the end of the 1st century and author of 1 Clement, a letter to the church at Corinth that is one of the earliest Christian writings outside the New Testament. He is one of the "Apostolic Fathers," the earliest Christian writers after the New Testament (c. 90–150).

What they did

  • Wrote 1 Clement (c. 96) on behalf of the church of Rome, urging the church at Corinth to restore the presbyters (elders) it had removed and to end its divisions.[1][3]
  • His letter is the earliest surviving witness outside the New Testament to the martyrdoms of the apostles Peter and Paul.[1][3]
  • Is named by Irenaeus (c. 180) as the third bishop of Rome after Peter, following Linus and Anacletus. Many historians think Rome was then led by a group of presbyters, with Clement as a leading figure and secretary.[2][3]
  • His letter was so respected that it was still read publicly in the church at Corinth around 170, and it was copied into the 5th-century Codex Alexandrinus with the New Testament.[2][1]
  • Is venerated as a saint and early pope, with a feast day on 23 November in the West.[5]

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of Clement of Rome supporting or carrying out persecution of others. His letter to the Corinthians (c. 96) urged the church to restore its deposed presbyters and called on the leaders of the dispute to submit or withdraw, an appeal within the church rather than a call for punishment.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Nothing reliable is known about how Clement died. The earliest writers, such as Irenaeus and Eusebius, do not say he was martyred. A legend first written down in the 4th century says he was exiled and martyred under Trajan. Some historians think the legend may confuse him with Titus Flavius Clemens, a Roman consul executed by Emperor Domitian in 95.

Excommunications

No excommunication or condemnation of him is recorded.

c. 99–101 (according to legend)

Exile to the Crimea and martyrdom (legend)

According to the 4th-century Acts of Clement, Emperor Trajan exiled Clement to work in the stone quarries near Chersonesus in the Crimea. There, the legend says, he converted many prisoners, and he was then thrown into the Black Sea with an anchor tied to his neck. This story is not found in earlier sources and is regarded as legend.[3][4]
