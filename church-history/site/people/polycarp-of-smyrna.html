Polycarp of Smyrna

c. 69–c. 155 · Early Church

Also known as Saint Polycarp

Bishop of Smyrna; martyr

Bishop of Smyrna, said by Irenaeus to have been taught by the apostle John, whose execution in old age is recorded in the Martyrdom of Polycarp, the earliest surviving account of a Christian martyrdom after the New Testament. He is one of the "Apostolic Fathers," the earliest Christian writers after the New Testament (c. 90–150).

What they did

  • Is described by his pupil Irenaeus as a disciple of the apostle John and of others who had seen Jesus, making him a living link between the apostles and the later 2nd-century church.[3][4]
  • Wrote a Letter to the Philippians, full of quotations from New Testament writings, which also preserves Ignatius's letters for the church at Philippi.[1][5]
  • Led the church in Smyrna for decades and received a letter from Ignatius of Antioch addressed to him personally.[1][6]
  • Traveled to Rome in old age to discuss with Bishop Anicetus the different dates on which churches celebrated Easter. They did not agree, but stayed in communion, and Anicetus let him celebrate the Eucharist in Rome.[4][3]
  • Opposed the teacher Marcion, reportedly calling him "the firstborn of Satan."[3]
  • His martyrdom account is the first to describe Christians keeping the anniversary of a martyr's death and honoring his remains, the beginning of the cult of the saints.[2][5]

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Polycarp was arrested during an outbreak of persecution in Smyrna, in which other Christians had already been killed by wild beasts. He refused to swear by the emperor's "genius" and curse Christ, and was burned and then stabbed in the stadium. The Martyrdom of Polycarp, written by the church of Smyrna, blames the crowd of both pagans and Jews. Scholars note that this account reflects the tensions of its time, and they debate how far its picture of Jewish involvement is accurate.

Excommunications

No excommunication or condemnation of him is recorded. His disagreement with Bishop Anicetus of Rome over the date of Easter did not lead to any break in communion.

23 February, c. 155–156

Arrest, trial, and execution in Smyrna

After the crowd in the stadium demanded that Polycarp be found, the police captain Herod sent men who found him at a farm outside the city. Brought before the proconsul, he refused to swear by the emperor's genius or to revile Christ. The crowd asked the Asiarch Philip to set a lion on him, but he refused because the animal games were over. The crowd then demanded that he be burned. According to the account, the flames formed an arch around him without harming him, so an executioner stabbed him, and his body was then burned.[2][5][4]
