Monica

c. 331–387 · Early Church

Also known as Saint Monica; Monnica

Christian mother of Augustine of Hippo; venerated as a saint and patron of mothers

North African Christian whose years of prayers and tears for her son Augustine, recorded in his Confessions, made her the model of the praying Christian mother.

Born
c. 331–332 · Thagaste, Numidia (modern Souk Ahras, Algeria)[1][2]
Died
Autumn 387, aged 56 · Ostia, near Rome[1][2]

What they did

  • Raised her children as Christians in a household where her husband Patricius was a pagan with a violent temper; Augustine records that she won him over by patience and that he was baptized before his death.[1][2]
  • Prayed and wept for years for Augustine while he followed the Manichees and lived with a concubine; a bishop she begged to talk to him told her, 'It is not possible that the son of these tears should perish.'[1][2]
  • Followed Augustine across the sea to Italy (385) and attached herself to Ambrose's church in Milan, giving up her African custom of bringing food and wine to the martyrs' tombs when Ambrose forbade it.[1][2]
  • Arranged a respectable marriage for Augustine in Milan, which led to his sending away the woman who had been his partner for about 15 years and the mother of his son Adeodatus; Augustine wrote that his heart 'was torn and wounded'.[1][2]
  • Took part in the philosophical conversations at Cassiciacum (386), where Augustine records her contributions in his dialogues.[2]
  • Shared with Augustine the 'vision at Ostia' while they waited to sail home to Africa, one of the most famous passages in the Confessions.[1]
  • Became, through the Confessions, the model of the persevering Christian mother. Her relics were moved to Rome in 1430 and the city of Santa Monica, California, is named after her.[3][4]
  • As a girl she secretly drank wine from the cellar until a servant called her a 'little drunkard' (meribibula), an episode Augustine includes to show her growth in virtue.[1]

Words and sayings

Her words are known only through Augustine's Confessions.

  • Lay this body anywhere; do not let care for it trouble you. This only I ask, that you remember me at the altar of the Lord, wherever you may be. Augustine, Confessions 9.11.27[1]

Misattributed 1

Commonly credited to them, but not found in their writings.

  • It is not possible that the son of these tears should perish. Confessions 3.12.21[1]

    Spoken to Monica by an unnamed African bishop, not by her; it is often wrongly credited to Ambrose.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Monica lived after the end of the Roman persecutions and was never arrested or persecuted. She did witness Ambrose's congregation occupying its basilica in Milan in 386 to resist Empress Justina's demand that it be handed to the Arians; Augustine says she was among those keeping watch.

Excommunications

Monica was never excommunicated or censured by any church authority.
