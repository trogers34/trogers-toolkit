Amphilochius of Iconium

c. 340–c. 400 · Early Church

Also known as Saint Amphilochius

Bishop of Iconium; cousin of Gregory of Nazianzus and friend of Basil the Great

A lawyer turned ascetic who, with Basil's support, became the first metropolitan bishop of Iconium in 373. He was a close ally of the Cappadocian Fathers: Basil dedicated On the Holy Spirit to him and addressed his Canonical Letters to him. He defended the divinity of the Holy Spirit, took part in the Council of Constantinople (381), and later led the condemnation of the Messalians.

What they did

  • Defended the full divinity of the Holy Spirit, prompting Basil's treatise On the Holy Spirit (375), which Basil dedicated to him.[1][6]
  • Received Basil's three Canonical Letters, which became part of Eastern church law on penance, marriage, and discipline.[1][6]
  • Wrote the Iambics to Seleucus, a poem of advice that includes an influential list of the books of Scripture; it was long attributed to Gregory of Nazianzus.[4][6]
  • Organized the church of Lycaonia as its first metropolitan and was one of the leading bishops of the Nicene party in Asia Minor.[5][6]
  • Was a cousin of Gregory of Nazianzus, who wrote him several letters, and a close friend of Basil.[2][5]

Persecution they supported or carried out

Amphilochius did not use force himself, but he pressed the emperor Theodosius to act against the Arians and presided over a synod that excommunicated the Messalians.

c. 383

Pressed Theodosius to restrict the Arians

Called for it

Against: Arians (Homoians and Eunomians)

Theodoret tells the story that Amphilochius, received at court, greeted Theodosius but pointedly ignored his young son Arcadius, then explained that God likewise expects the Son to be honoured equally with the Father; Theodosius is said to have responded by issuing a law forbidding Arian assemblies. The story's details are not confirmed elsewhere.[3][5]

Outcome: Laws of the early 380s restricted Arian worship, though the link to this meeting rests on Theodoret.

c. 390

Condemnation of the Messalians at Side

Took part

Against: Messalians (an ascetic movement that taught constant prayer over the sacraments)

He presided over a synod at Side in Pamphylia that condemned the Messalians and excommunicated their leaders; they were later condemned again at Antioch and at the Council of Ephesus (431).[6][5]

Outcome: The Messalians were excluded from communion in the region.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

No persecution of Amphilochius is recorded. He became bishop under the anti-Nicene emperor Valens but was not exiled.

Excommunications

Amphilochius was never excommunicated or condemned. He is honoured as a saint in the Orthodox and Catholic churches.
